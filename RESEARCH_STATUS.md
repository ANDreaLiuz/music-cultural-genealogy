# Research status

**Research in progress · Public summary prepared 28 October 2026. No new independently validated musical-lineage finding is claimed after the previously frozen exploratory work.**

This project explores musical relationships at the level of individual recordings. Its earlier engineering prototypes combined audio descriptors with neighborhood graphs, Leiden community assignments and a separately computed Ward hierarchy. The current research framing asks which relationships are meaningful and how they could be validated independently.

| Evidence layer | Current boundary |
|---|---|
| Exploratory computation | Local prototypes and later computational audits exist. Their data, source code and numerical findings are withheld from this public overview. |
| Perceptual validity | Independent human validation on the project's exact current materials remains pending. External human datasets are separate secondary evidence. |
| Natural preference change | A validated longitudinal test linking independently measured relations, repeated liking and actual listening opportunities remains future work. |
| Historical influence | No validated historical descent tree is claimed. Audio proximity alone cannot establish influence. |
| Causal preference guidance | Not established. No causal intervention result is claimed. |
| Publications | Research in progress. No submitted, accepted or published paper is claimed. |

The accompanying graph is a newly authored synthetic teaching illustration. It supplies no evidence about real music, human perception or recommendation quality.

The project remains exploratory. Parameter sensitivity, recording identity, artist and production confounds, temporal context, uncertain relationships and appropriate baselines are material parts of the research question.
