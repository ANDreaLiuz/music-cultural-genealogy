# Research questions

The unit of interest is an individual, accurately identified recording. Artist and genre can provide context, but cannot substitute for a direct relationship between recordings.

1. Which aspects of musical relationship can be measured reproducibly: timbre and production, harmony, rhythmic organization, melodic organization or development over time?
2. How much do a representation, its normalization, the excerpt and the distance measure change the resulting neighborhood?
3. When are communities useful exploratory summaries, and when do continuous, overlapping or uncertain relationships provide a better description?
4. Could independently measured relationships add information about future preference change beyond prior liking, familiarity, exposure, artist, genre, acoustics and time?

The fourth question requires independent relationship measurements, repeated liking, actual candidate opportunities and future held-out evaluation. A recommendation interface or a sequence of plays does not by itself answer it.

The long-term cultural question also needs historical evidence. Similarity cannot determine who influenced whom, whether a musical practice was transmitted or whether two recordings share a common source.
